# Scope, provenance, and responsibility

ASTRA is the project’s acronym for **Astronomical State-Topology and Reservoir Analysis**. SPPT is **Solar-Planetary Phase-Partition Theory**, the associated reservoir-network description. The name ASTRA also echoes *Ad Astra Per Aspera* — “To the Stars Through Difficulties” — as a general motto. That naming history is not a claim of affiliation, endorsement, priority, or exclusive rights.

## Two different kinds of status

Publication pages use four plain-language labels:

- **Current**
- **Working paper**
- **Draft**
- **Archive**

Scientific claims use a different vocabulary:

- **reported** — attributed to a named source or observation;
- **derived under stated assumptions** — obtained algebraically or computationally inside an explicit model;
- **inferred** — supported through a declared reconstruction or observation model;
- **proposed** — a hypothesis, method, or experiment design awaiting a stronger test;
- **unknown** — not established by the available evidence; and
- **rejected** — ruled out only within the stated claim, model, or parameter boundary.

These labels do not transfer authority. A published Working paper can contain proposed and unknown claims. A mechanically verified derivation can still await independent review. A negative result is retained rather than edited into a success.

## Scientific scope

The core project studies constrained reservoir networks, hidden connectivity, state-dependent transport, observation operators, identifiability, and held-out comparison. Its numerical demonstrations are reduced and synthetic. Supplemental papers explore related methods under their own boundaries.

The Dark-Medium Response Atlas distinguishes a gravitational deficit from collective plasma response and from preferred-frame claims. It tests possible overlaps and discriminators; it does not identify those roles as one substance or report a dark-matter detection. Its gravitational-pulse, microwave-synthesis, and Centaur cases transfer inference structure only.
