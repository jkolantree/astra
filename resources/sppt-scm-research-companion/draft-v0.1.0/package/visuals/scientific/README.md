# Scientific Visual Atlas

Six original scientific figures and two actual animations accompany *Physics Synthesis and Research Priorities for ASTRA SPPT and SCM*, research edition dated 5 October 2026.

## Start here

- `CAPTIONS_AND_SOURCES.md`: full captions, evidence limits and primary-source links.
- `manifest.json`: relative asset paths, numerical parameters, units, provenance and SHA-256 hashes.
- `figures/`: publication-size PNGs (2160 × 1368) and editable SVG versions.
- `animations/`: looping GIFs, H.264 MP4s and PNG poster frames (1100 × 704).
- `generate_scientific_atlas.py`: the complete original, standalone asset generator.

The figures are conceptual schematics, analytic geometry or conditional model predictions using synthetic inputs. None is an empirical dataset, experimental replication, spacecraft-image reconstruction or validation of a common physical dynamics. The planetary and geometric diagrams do not identify SCM with planetary fluid or plasma laws. The quantum plot credits the established partial-transpose spectrum in a September 2026 preprint. This is an unpromoted research companion; it does not change the stable science authority.

## Reproduce

From this directory, run:

```sh
python generate_scientific_atlas.py
```

Trusted installed dependencies used for the delivered build:

- Python 3.12
- NumPy 2.3.5
- Matplotlib 3.10.8
- Pillow 12.3.0
- FFmpeg with libx264 (optional, for MP4 exports)

No network requests, research-package imports or archived research scripts are involved. All curves, diagrams and animation frames are generated by the included script. If FFmpeg is absent, the PNG, SVG and GIF outputs still generate. Matplotlib and fontconfig caches use the operating system’s temporary directory. Font choice is DejaVu Sans. A fixed SVG hash salt, fixed random verification seed and fixed GIF palette are used.

SVG labels, lines and schematic shapes are editable. The dense field in the quantum map is embedded as a raster layer inside its SVG; edit its formula or parameter grid in the generator to regenerate it without altering its labeled axes. No source-paper figure is copied or traced.

The reproduction also creates `validation.json` and a `qa/` directory with all lossless animation frames and full-frame contact sheets. Numerical checks compare negativity against independently assembled 4 × 4 density matrices and partial transposes, verify normal-frame and projection orthonormality, and check discrete WKI growth controls. These are computational consistency checks, not peer review or a proof of physical validity.

## Animation scope and viewing

- A1 lasts 10 seconds at 10 frames/s. The blue ten-lobed trace advances one wavelength while orange markers make two circuits. Rates and radii are arbitrary display parameters; marker motion is prescribed rather than solved from fluid advection.
- A2 lasts 8 seconds at 10 frames/s. A helix’s amplitude is varied smoothly, and its analytic normal frame is recalculated. Display time does not represent a dynamical solution.

Both use constant backgrounds and colors, continuous motion and no deliberate flashes. This design choice is not a medical photosensitivity certification. PNG poster frames provide a motion-free alternative. The GIF files loop; the MP4 files play one cycle unless a player repeats them.

## Source-access boundaries

The source report and its final planetary addendum were inspected directly. Primary public sources were checked where accessible. Source-access qualifications are preserved in each caption. In particular, the slow-freezing Enceladus final methods/supplement and exact selected-event list were not recovered; the rapid-decompression source is a conference abstract; the older WKI transformed-background stability comparison remains unresolved. The animation about Saturn uses an institutional release only for motivation, with no claimed reproduction of measured speeds or a fitted mechanism.
